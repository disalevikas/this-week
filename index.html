How it works

Every Monday, This Week asks a single question: what is this week for?

You answer in one line. For the rest of the week, that line is all your new tab shows. When the week turns, it asks whether it happened — and records your answer without scoring it.

That's the whole thing.

Why a week

A day is too short to mean anything and a year is too long to feel. A week is the unit a life is actually lived in — and if you live to eighty, you get about four thousand of them.

The idea comes from Oliver Burkeman's book Four Thousand Weeks. This extension is an independent project and is not affiliated with the author or publisher.

What it does

  • Asks one question a week, and shows your answer back all week
  • Lets you name what the week is not for
  • Closes the loop: it happened, some of it, or it didn't
  • Keeps an archive of what you said mattered
  • Optional life-in-weeks grid
  • Exports everything as plain text

What it refuses to do

  • No streaks, no completion rate, no percentages
  • No to-do list — one line a week, not a backlog
  • No quotes, clock, weather, or widgets
  • No nagging; leaving a week open is a real answer
  • Never steals focus from the address bar

Discreet mode

Built for open-plan desks.

The entries most worth writing are the ones you least want read over your shoulder. Turn on discreet mode and the page stays blank — not blurred, blank, which looks no different from a week you left open. Hold Shift, or hover the small label, to read it.

The first new tab each morning shows the week plainly. Every tab after that is blank. The archive is covered too.

Privacy

Nothing you write leaves your browser.

One permission: storage. No network requests of any kind — not analytics, not fonts, not error reporting. There is no account to create and no server to trust. Your entries sync between your own Chrome profiles through Chrome's built-in sync, and "erase everything" in settings deletes all of it.

Install

Get it on the Chrome Web Store

Replaces your new tab page. Remove it any time from chrome://extensions and Chrome restores the default.

Questions

Does it track me or send anything anywhere?

No. The extension makes no network requests at all. It has a single permission, storage, used only to save what you type so it can be shown back to you.

What happens if I skip a week?

Nothing. "Leave this week open" is a first-class answer and it sticks — the page won't ask again that week. There are no streaks to break.

Can I get my data out?

Yes. The archive exports as a plain text file at any time, and everything can be erased from settings in one click.

Does it work offline?

Entirely. It has never needed a connection.

Why is the page so empty?

Because you open a new tab about a hundred times a day, and anything placed there becomes wallpaper within a week. The emptiness is what keeps the one line worth reading.
